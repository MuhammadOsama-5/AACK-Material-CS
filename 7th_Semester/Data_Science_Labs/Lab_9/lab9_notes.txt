Task 1 - Estimate Housing Prices Near Transit (task1_housing.py)
==================================================================
Data: UCI Real Estate Valuation, 414 rows.
Feature: distance to nearest MRT station in units of 100 m.
Target: price per unit area (10,000 NT$ per ping).

(a) Slope:     -0.7262 price units per 100 m
    Intercept: 45.8514 price units

(b) Predicted price at 500 m (distance_100m = 5): 42.22 price units

(c) The slope is negative, so price goes down as the house gets farther
    from the MRT station. Each extra 100 m lowers the predicted price by
    about 0.73 units, which is about 7,262 NT$ per ping. For example, a
    house 1 km farther away is predicted to cost about 7.3 units less
    per ping. The plot (task1_housing.png) shows the points dropping
    quickly near the station and the straight line following that trend.

==================================================================
Task 2 - Compare Models for Yacht Resistance (task2_yacht.py)
==================================================================
Data: UCI Yacht Hydrodynamics, 308 rows.
Feature: Froude number (column 5). Target: residuary resistance (column 6).
Same 5 shuffled folds (random_state=42), negative RMSE scoring.

Model        Fold RMSEs                          Mean
Linear       8.16  10.57  9.84  8.38  7.78       8.95
Quadratic    3.93   4.59  4.45  4.49  3.50       4.19

Better model: Quadratic. Its mean CV RMSE (4.19) is less than half of the
linear model's (8.95), and it wins on every fold. Resistance rises sharply
at higher speed, so a curved line fits much better than a straight one.
Note: this row-based split can put experiments from the same hull in
different folds, so the scores may be a bit optimistic.

==================================================================
Task 3 - Tune a Concrete Strength Model (task3_concrete.py)
==================================================================
Data: UCI Concrete Compressive Strength, 1005 rows after removing
duplicates. 804 training rows and 201 test rows (random_state=42).

(a) Linear baseline fold RMSEs: 10.10  10.18  10.05  10.18  10.49
    Linear baseline mean CV RMSE: 10.20 MPa

(b) Grid search over degree [1, 2, 3] and alpha [0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]
    Top 3 settings:
      Rank 1: degree=3, alpha=0.1   mean CV RMSE = 6.09 MPa
      Rank 2: degree=3, alpha=0.01  mean CV RMSE = 6.16 MPa
      Rank 3: degree=3, alpha=1     mean CV RMSE = 6.27 MPa
    Best: degree=3, alpha=0.1

(c) Refitted winner on the held-out test set:
      Test RMSE: 6.52 MPa
      Test MAE:  4.67 MPa
    The plot (task3_concrete.png) shows most points close to the y = x
    line, with bigger misses at high strengths.

(d) The tuned model lowers mean CV RMSE from 10.20 to 6.09 MPa, an
    improvement of about 4.11 MPa. The test RMSE (6.52) is close to the
    CV RMSE, so the model is not badly overfitting.

    Interaction term example: Cement x Water. It lets the effect of
    cement depend on how much water is in the mix (the water-cement
    ratio matters a lot for strength).

    Why scaling goes inside the pipeline: Polynomial terms have very
    different sizes, and Ridge penalizes all coefficients equally, so
    features must be scaled first. Putting StandardScaler inside the
    pipeline means it is fitted only on the training folds in each CV
    round, so no information from the validation fold or test set
    leaks into the model.
